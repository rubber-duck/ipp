# Rendering and Interaction

[Architecture overview](../architecture.md) · [Implementation strategy](../plans/runtime-and-rendering.md#rendering-and-recovery)

## Shared render service

Each World's selected Systems prepare completed, versioned output; the Host's RenderService composes it and owns GPU execution. Contexts/surfaces, connections and Worlds have independent lifetimes. Submissions consume immutable derived geometry, material inputs, palettes, lights and picking state without reading mutable components, including when a branch is frozen. Rendering never advances simulation or reevaluates geometry. GPU handles, programs and packing stay outside persistent World state; shader source belongs to immutable assets.

Current GL composition supports one context per service, multiple Worlds and synchronous submission. Independent contexts over one catalog require further representation support. [Asset providers](assets.md#shader-recipes-and-graphics-loading) own graphics loading and readiness; [recovery](assets.md#retention-and-recovery) preserves immutable identity and usable CPU data.

## Outputs and composition

An OutputRef explicitly selects an output of a runtime World: the World's canvas, or a Camera entity's view. A canvas output names no entity; it exists while the World lives and selects the Canvas System, and it is fenced by the World's lifetime. A Camera output is fenced by its entity and Camera component lifetime. Host root views and Surface attachments use the same output selection contract; opening an authoring session cannot select a view. A World has one active presentation path in this scope, without simultaneous instancing. An invalidated output selection stays unavailable until explicitly corrected; there is no camera fallback. Canvas and Camera are distinct outputs, composed through explicit nested attachments rather than an implicit overlay on every scene.

Root configuration and actual surface selection are distinct. One graphics surface selects one exact root binding independently of authoring sessions; other configured roots remain alive without being drawn into that surface. Every successful explicit root bind, including an equal-value bind, has a fresh Host-fenced generation. Conditional cleanup compares that generation and cannot clear a replacement. Surface selection and context replacement have their own lifetimes. Drawing dimensions must equal the acknowledged binding viewport; device limits are negotiated before selection, never silently clamped during drawing. A selected surface resizes its root in one request that rebinds the same output with the new viewport and continues the selection, so no draw falls between the rebind and the selection and retained presentation state survives a resize; the previous binding and view become stale as after a separate bind and selection.

Presentation completion acknowledges an actual successful selected draw, not root configuration, World evaluation, an invalid-camera clear or a headless no-op. Its immutable stamp names the surface/context/selection, root generation, output, source publication, viewport and presentation sequence. An exact-publication request constrains a current authorized draw; it never authorizes historical replay and fails boundedly when obsolete or unavailable. Pending frame/readback requests revalidate these fences at completion. Completed CPU captures remain immutable snapshots across later rebinds or context loss, without claiming current display authority; their connection-owned transfers follow the common [bulk-read acknowledgement, release and pressure rules](protocol-and-schema.md#host-to-client-bulk-reads). Pending waits and retained captures are bounded per connection first, by count and capture bytes; Host-wide bounds are derived from those allowances for a few presenting connections, so one connection at its allowance cannot exhaust presentation capacity for another. A request over a bound fails with a capacity error scoped to that request. None of these operations advances simulation time.

A completion request may additionally require included content from exact child or root outputs after Host admission. Admission freezes output lifetimes and their next evaluation cuts; replacement never retargets a pending request. Completion observes the actual composed draw, including image provenance: an unchanged retained image can represent a later equivalent publication, but stale cached content cannot be relabeled current. This bounded observation does not force evaluation, repaint or cache-policy changes. It establishes post-admission presentation, not the first visible frame of an action, pixel visibility through occlusion or physical display scanout. The [renderer contract](../../crates/ipp-render-gl/README.md#output-inclusion) explains the implementation boundary.

[Spatial attachments](runtime.md#world-attachments) contribute child-local scene content through their attachment entity's transform into the containing camera and depth domain. Merge draws, lights, shadow casters/receivers and transparency ordering across that domain, using World-qualified identities and attachment-transformed bounds, light frames, palettes and particle depths. Attachment bounds support conservative culling, not hard volume clipping. Spatial children do not implicitly display their canvas or select another camera.

SurfaceCanvas presents the child World's canvas through the selected [Surface implementation](#surface-presentation), using direct primitives or derived images with optional temporal caching. SurfaceCamera renders the selected child view into a color/depth target and composites its linear-color result through the same Surface mapping. Each camera output defines a separate rendering domain; nested targets prepare before their consumers. Blend linearly within and between outputs, performing display conversion only at the root. Frozen contributions follow the [frame contract](runtime.md#frame-order) and [publication resource leases](assets.md#retention-and-recovery).

## Compile-time GL devices

WebGL 2 and GLES 3 share preparation through compile-time devices. Bindings/dialects stay behind that boundary; Hosts own context libraries/shims. Future non-GL backends preserve World contracts without implementing the GL interface. Validate actual device capabilities; unsupported requirements need explicit errors or defined fallbacks.

## Material contract

Intended V1 scope includes unlit and forward metallic-roughness PBR, image-based lighting, directional/point/spot lights, spotlight shadows, skinning and optional MSAA. [Current scope](../development/building.md#toolchain-and-scope) distinguishes delivery.

Materials/lights are ordinary components; instance edits never mutate shared assets. Unlit/debug rendering is independent of lighting. Meshes retain supplied attributes only; required missing streams fail and optional streams use defined defaults. [Immutable recipes](assets.md#shader-recipes-and-graphics-loading) own program identity; disabled features omit snippets/composition.

Deformation proceeds from mesh-pose interpolation through skinning to final object placement, consistently across surface/depth passes and conservative headless bounds. Shadow, forward, resolve and presentation remain separate responsibilities.

## Lights and shadows

Select bounded influential lights per draw from the containing rendering domain's unrestricted lights, including spatial descendants. Use trustworthy evaluated bounds and instanced group bounds, stable selections near the cutoff and deterministic World/entity ties. Unknown bounds permit approximate ranking, never geometric rejection. Selection/history remain renderer state.

Allocate device-bounded shadows across the frame, prioritizing lights selected by visible draws and mapping draw indices to stable atlas slots. Unallocated lights still illuminate. Allocation failure reduces shadow participation with diagnostics without failing the World/session.

## Custom materials

Custom materials own dynamic properties independently of immutable backend-specific shader definitions. Definitions validate required names/types, permit extra properties and supply standardized interfaces without a generic shader-language translator. Omitted vertex bodies use standard deformation; missing fragments make that backend unavailable. Custom vertices own clip-space output and must preserve surface/shadow deformation through pass-specific transforms and shared helpers.

Select usable materials in custom → PBR → unlit order, with solid unlit red as the final custom fallback. Missing resources, unsupported requirements and compilation failures preserve authored state and report diagnostics. Surface and requested shadow passes use the same candidate; programs/failures follow the asset lifecycle independently of values.

Materials declare opaque, cutout or blended rendering and optional light/shadow participation. Lighting supplies inputs without imposing a shading model. Opaque/cutout draws write depth and group by shader/material state, with front-to-back depth inside each group. Particle instances retain their submission groups. Blended draws only depth-test and sort back-to-front across the composed domain with stable World/entity ties. Blend linearly before one display conversion. Blended custom materials do not cast shadows; cutout/discard applies to shadow passes.

Arbitrary vertex deformation disables culling unless authored bounds promise conservative enclosure. Picking still uses authored geometry. The [material guide](../../crates/ipp-render-gl/src/services/render/CUSTOM_MATERIALS.md) owns shader interfaces and packing.

## Cameras

Camera entities hold headless projection state; explicit [output references](#outputs-and-composition) select their views. Unusable selection clears a root view or makes a nested output unavailable and fails dependent queries. Selection changes at safe presentation boundaries; removal never selects a replacement. Valid edits/selections recover, and invalid projection preserves the selection/session.

Rendering, picking, projection and navigation name their camera/view target explicitly. Projection dimensions belong to the view: root views use their current viewport and headless callers supply dimensions. A nested Camera uses its containing Surface’s physical aspect ratio; render-target rounding and device limits affect resolution, never projection or input geometry. Rendering/picking share completed camera and geometry state. Clients interpret gestures; Rust navigation writes camera/transform fields under ordinary partial-mutation rules, never feeding evaluated state back into authoring. Stale output identities fail rather than redirecting an operation.

## Bounding and picking geometry

Shared headless geometry supports Box, Sphere, Pill, placed compound unions and skeletal mappings.

| Component | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| `BoundingGeometry` | Conservative enclosure including deformation/scaling; required for culling rejection |
| `PickingGeometry` | Independent interaction shapes; presence enables picking |

Neither substitutes for the other. [Immutable definitions](assets.md#geometry-definitions) may be shared; instances own final-pose shapes, including joint-pair pills. Skeleton replacement invalidates mappings before ordinals can be reused.

Renderable presentation components require BoundingGeometry; a missing one is inserted with defaults under the [required-component rule](runtime.md#required-components). Unknown or unproven bounds remain eligible for rendering, including pending resources and arbitrary vertex deformation. Particle bounds enclose the evaluated effect.

Geometry components retain derived world bounds beside their evaluated shapes. GeometrySystem owns spatial indexing over those results and publishes queries after final geometry evaluation. Consumers bind prepared geometry access rather than deriving enclosures independently. Bounding and picking query domains preserve their separate contracts.

Spatial queries accept batches and return conservative geometry/query matches into reusable caller storage. Index topology and traversal order are implementation details; object identity and order-sensitive tie-breaking remain independent. Frustum traversal narrows active-query bitmasks uniformly, including single-bit masks. Flat scans and spatial hierarchies share the query contract; backend selection and rebuilding occur outside active queries. Unknown render bounds remain explicit candidates rather than disappearing from an index-only traversal.

## Geometry picking

Picking is independent of renderer, materials, visibility and visualization. Missing required geometry fails rather than reporting hit/miss. Composed queries transform rays through spatial attachments and Surface output mappings, compare distances in the containing view and return World-qualified targets and attachment paths with deterministic ties. Results identify their view/publication and participating evaluation revisions; a latest-state query does not imply a historical displayed frame. Projection/view-plane queries support client-owned gestures without core drag state. CPU triangle picking is excluded; future GPU picking answers rendered-surface occupancy.

## Global render state

Settings use atomic sparse updates and independent [change events](protocol-and-schema.md#system-commands-queries-and-events). Omission preserves fields; rejection/no-op emits nothing. The containing camera domain supplies clear, ambient and output settings; spatial descendants do not override them. Uniform ambient fill defaults to zero and affects PBR independently of punctual lighting. It is distinct from image-based lighting and leaves unlit/debug rendering unaffected. Display overrides preserve authored components and explicit output selection.

## Geometry visualization

Bounding/picking components opt into visualization in the selected camera view, with normal depth and unlit color; no separate debug component exists. Global overrides affect presentation only. The renderer privately owns demanded debug assets, without client resource records/events. Recovery regenerates them; allocation failure skips affected draws. Declarations remain valid headlessly; visualization is standard rendering functionality.

## Residency and recovery

Visibility/quality select variant demand from presented publications; frozen content remains a presentation consumer and current parent views may change its quality selection. Aggregate residency for observation without asset quotas. [Assets](assets.md#retention-and-recovery) own CPU/source leases, revocation and GPU recovery requirements. Allocation failure skips affected draws while preserving other resources; retry after unload or context restoration.

Context loss suspends GPU work and restores programs/context state before demanded resources under the [immutable recovery contract](assets.md#retention-and-recovery). Resource/draw, context, World-evaluation and fatal Host failures have distinct scopes. Recoverable failures skip affected work or use fallback without destroying the Host; context loss reaches recovery even from material preparation. Validate allocations and resource uploads when they complete, and check GL errors at the end of passes whose results are kept. Frame ends check on a sampled subset of frames and after retained-storage replacement, so other errors may be attributed to a later frame, while context loss is still detected within the frame it occurs. Explicit exhaustive checks attribute each error to its failing call, with the [hot-path logging policy](runtime.md#diagnostic-logging) unchanged.

## World and color conventions

| Domain | Convention |
| --- | --- |
| World | Right-handed; +X right, +Y up, camera-forward −Z; metres/radians |
| Canvas content | Top-left origin; +X right, +Y down; logical units mapped through canvas density |
| Surface content | Top-left origin; +X right, +Y down; metres |
| Transforms | Translation, xyzw quaternion, scale; column vectors/matrices; local `T × R × S` |
| Faces | Counter-clockwise front faces |
| Import/backend | Explicit foreign-coordinate and clip-space conversion |
| Color | Linear RGB with sRGB primaries; linear coverage alpha; explicit texture/output conversion |
| Initial opaque output | Linear RGB → sRGB, without tone mapping |

## Particle presentation

Sprites own unlit appearance/alignment; particle meshes use immutable geometry and sibling materials, including custom materials. GL owns instance buffers and packing outside schemas/cache formats. Default vertices specialize ordinary/instanced inputs; custom vertices implement the instancing interface, with shared parameters per effect.

Alpha particles participate in scene transparency order with deterministic ties, splitting batches as needed; additive particles need no depth sorting. Bounds must enclose the effect rather than its source mesh. Particle picking is outside initial scope.

## Data visualization

The optional Plot System consumes each entity's data-source binding under the [data-binding contract](data.md#data-bindings-and-evaluation) and owns visual axes, series and derived presentation without per-point entities. Plot maps evaluated columns to display dimensions independently of source ingestion. 2D plots use shared Canvas primitives; 3D plots use ordinary scene geometry.

3D axis, label and tick presentation belongs to Plot components that are themselves renderable presentation components. They draw through the same plane mapping, clipping, scene depth, glyph and path facilities as [Surface](#surface-presentation): a shared plane renderer, not a shared or intermediate component. No child World is involved, and no component writes another component's content. Axis, tick and annotation text faces the selected camera at its retained world/data anchor, with ordinary scene depth; annotation panels and connectors share that facing plane. The selected view arranges projected text and panel bounds with bounded deterministic local offsets, keeping titles distinct from tick lanes and retaining connectors to the data anchors. Cartesian grids select the far bounds of the data enclosure in each view while retaining scene orientation.

Axes choose visible boundary edges from the selected camera and retained chart content, preserving increasing coordinate direction. They prefer standard positions, leave only when visibility becomes poor and require a higher visibility threshold to return, so small camera movements do not alternate the chosen edge. Each axis line, ticks and title move together when that choice changes, following the enclosure faces through adjacent corners, by default over two Host-clock seconds, rather than crossing the data volume. RenderService retains and retargets this derived transition state per view and chart lifetime; it uses Host-supplied presentation time without writing authored state, advancing the World or rebuilding local geometry. Numeric text retains its projected station in one compact outward lane. Crowded views omit overlapping intermediate tick labels instead of displacing them, favor readable endpoints and reveal labels again when space permits.

Pie callouts extend outward along projected slice bisectors, with ordered radial spacing and connectors attached to panel edges. Chart geometry remains fixed. These view-dependent placements never rebuild binding results or local mesh/glyph streams.

Initial 2D presentation supports lines, bars and pies. Initial 3D presentation supports pies with data-defined slice radius, colour and height; grid-positioned bars, including a single row; height surfaces over two coordinates; and separate point plots for disconnected samples. Histogram binning belongs to client analysis, whose results can feed ordinary bars. Source and data-binding contracts remain independent of these representations.

2D line charts offer straight or smooth interpolation from the same source samples; clients supply neither extra samples nor control points. Interpolation preserves missing-data gaps and leaves source data unchanged. Plot derives line, arrow and 2D pie-sector outlines as paths for the existing analytic quadratic renderer, through the same runtime path generation smooth lines need. Styling may later come from [GUI skins](gui.md#text-and-skins), but Plot does not depend on new primitive shapes. Initial plotting uses the full prepared sample set without automatic data reduction.

Each Cartesian plot initially uses one shared frame with linear axes and fixed bounds or automatic bounds from participating binding views. Series consume evaluated columns; pies use independent radial layout. Display bounds do not change source retention or binding windows. More elaborate axis and interaction contracts are outside this initial scope.

Legends are reusable client compositions of ordinary Canvas content, sharing caller-authored color encodings with the charts they describe. A scene legend uses an ordinary Surface attachment beside its chart; it does not introduce another data consumer or change Plot's preparation boundary.

Plot components own client-authored label rows containing a source-row identity, text and a highlighted flag, together with chart-specific layout and connector properties. The label array determines the authored labels. Labels are presentation state, independent of source schema and data-binding evaluation. Plot hits go through the existing [picking path](#geometry-picking) and report the hit series and source-row identity; the client decides the response, such as labels or highlight.

## Surface presentation

Surface is the headless contract for presenting a two-dimensional content domain in space. One concrete Surface component on an attachment entity supplies its geometry and physical content extent; FlatSurface preserves planar presentation, while CylinderSurface and SphereSurface present curved segments. The attachment independently selects one child World's canvas or Camera output. Text, positioned glyph runs, drawings and bitmaps remain ordinary Canvas entities; glyphs, vertices and control skin parts remain compact subsystem data.

Each Surface implementation owns its valid content domain, content-and-layer-offset mapping to entity-local positions and front normals, ray intersections with inverse content coordinates, conservative bounds and parameter validation. It provides geometry sampling sufficient for renderer-controlled approximation; an exact affine mapping permits direct planar presentation. Rendering, input and bounds consume the same completed geometry publication. Implementations are registered Rust capabilities with ordinary generated component schemas, included in native and WASM builds under the [workspace policy](rust-workspace.md); one entity has at most one concrete Surface provider. Component values remain the only authored state. Surface implementations are independent of GL devices, Canvas content and GUI control behavior; RenderService owns tessellation, images, GPU resources and quality policy.

FlatSurface maps the top-left, +X-right, +Y-down content rectangle to a centred local XY plane with +Z facing forward. CylinderSurface maps horizontal content distance to arc length and leaves its vertical axis straight. SphereSurface uses a centre-based equidistant mapping, with unavoidable distortion away from the centre. Curved implementations preserve the centre and tangent orientation, support inside- or outside-facing segments and a continuous flat limit, and keep one authoritative parameterization of extent and curvature; radius and angular-extent authoring helpers derive that parameterization. Validation excludes ambiguous domains and layer offsets that collapse or invert a shell. Entity transforms then place the geometry in the right-handed, +Y-up World. Source font/drawing conventions are normalized at the shared preparation boundary.

A World that selects the Canvas System is a canvas, and has exactly one. Every top-level entity and its descendants are canvas content, painted depth-first in core sibling order within each layer. The canvas's logical extent and units-per-metre density are Canvas System state, [set at World creation and changed by System command](runtime.md#system-composition); they are not components and are not animatable. Presentation supplies the extent: a Surface derives it from its physical size and the density through attachment context, a root viewport uses its own CSS-pixel extent and ignores density, and the stored extent applies only while the canvas is not presented. Scaling or clipping presented content belongs to the parent Surface or a scale component in the layout; content needing its own density or coordinate space is a separate child World on a Surface. Raw leaves and [GUI layout](gui.md#layout-and-presentation) share canvas-local placement, evaluated visual transforms, rectangular clips and painter order from the ordered entity tree, and may mix. Layout supplies positions for its managed children; explicitly positioned leaves use their authored placement. An optional CanvasStyle overrides an entity's placement, layer, tint, opacity and clip; without one the entity paints with the identity style, and there is no World-level default style. Translation and scale compose, clips intersect, and tint and opacity multiply down the tree, so a subtree is dimmed or coloured from its root. The Canvas System writes each laid-out entity's evaluated bounds to a separate system-written CanvasBounds component, which GUI controls and Canvas leaves require. Every Surface entity in a canvas World is a canvas slot placed by the same layout and paint ordering. The canvas and its slots appear only where the canvas is presented; a camera presenting that World draws only its 3D content, so a 3D World whose Surfaces are placed spatially does not select the Canvas System.

A canvas entity's non-animated CanvasStyle layer is a non-negative offset from its parent's resolved layer; zero inherits, including at the canvas root. A positive offset establishes a component layer root. Equal resolved levels share a layer across ordinary trees in the same canvas, independent of intervening zero-offset entities. Paint follows resolved layer and then core tree order; targeting reverses that order. A component's skin parts and decorative descendants share its root's layer unless explicitly raised. Ordinary layer roots retain inherited rectangular clips, translation, scale, tint and opacity. [GUI overlays](gui.md#controls-and-composites) establish separate priority and clipping scopes; layering alone changes neither layout nor keyboard traversal.

Shared headless presentation maps the ordered layer groups occupied by visible Canvas entities to consecutive depth ranks; structural layer roots count independently of whether they paint. A Surface's animatable layer spacing separates those ranks along the implementation's local front normals, producing parallel planes or concentric curved shells. Large authored offsets affect priority without creating physical gaps; adding or removing an occupied group may reposition later ranks. Placement is deterministic from the completed publication, with no history-dependent slot allocation. Positive spacing increases radius on outside-facing curved surfaces and decreases it on inside-facing ones; signed spacing can reverse the direction within the implementation's valid offset range. Renderer culling includes the occupied shells; the parent World's ordinary BoundingGeometry encloses the base Surface, independently of its child's layers.

A component may explicitly author a previous relative layer and a normalized transition progress alongside its current destination layer. An ordinary Host-owned animation driver animates progress; shared headless presentation interpolates the component's physical placement between the resolved endpoints through the Surface mapping. Its inherited decorations follow it, and descendant layer roots retain their relative layering. Rendering, bounds, clipping and input consume the same completed placement, including direct and image-based presentation. Logical ordering follows the destination; overlay priority and interaction scopes remain discrete. The previous endpoint is ordinary authored state, never an automatically saved value or historical slot. Removing it immediately uses the current layer and ordinary stack resolution; any resulting jump is intentional, with no automatic smoothing or continuity preservation.

Composed input intersects the occupied surfaces nearest first, checks local facing and the permitted content domain, and inverts the same mapping before testing the corresponding layer's eligible targets and clips. Coincident layers use reverse painter order. Captured input retains its target and follows its current completed layer placement; it can leave content rectangles and clips while a valid intersection exists, and a ray missing that geometry produces no invented movement. Only a Surface in a Camera's 3D domain separates layers or applies curved geometry. A root viewport uses ordering only; a nested canvas slot presents its child on the slot's layer, with the containing Surface supplying any curvature and physical separation. Child Canvas layers do not merge across World boundaries.

Content properties use ordinary component fields and animation. Core reordering preserves entity identity and bindings; removal invalidates affected access before reuse. Numeric animation retains prepared access rather than rebuilding unrelated content. Persistence follows ordinary entity/component ownership and retains typed asset references.

Basic string layout is headless; explicit glyph runs preserve client-owned shaping and positioning. Offline converters own font/SVG interpretation, while renderer-owned providers prepare GPU representations under the ordinary immutable asset lifecycle. RenderSystem submits evaluated Surface inputs independently of mesh submissions, and RenderService owns shape coverage, glyph presentation, bitmap sampling and GPU packing.

Surfaces render unlit and double-sided with scene depth testing; rear views show the presented content through its reverse side, while the implementation's local front normal determines interaction facing. Direct vector rendering uses presentation resolution. Content composes in explicit painter's order with batching limited to compatible contiguous work; separate Surfaces participate in ordinary scene transparency ordering. Rectangular clipping is evaluated in the two-dimensional content domain before its Surface mapping. Missing resources suppress only affected items, and context recovery preserves logical state. Raw Surface interaction uses the shared geometry and content-projection queries; [GUI interaction](gui.md#evaluation-and-input) additionally owns control hit testing and explicit scene blockers, independently of rendering.

### GUI shapes and retained presentation

Parameterized boxes are the primary geometry for ordinary GUI content. A box is a rectangle whose corners each take a radius or a 45° cut, with optional per-corner accent spans that draw the border thicker near a corner, or brackets alone over a zero border; a stroke mark of one or two straight segments, such as a check, cross or chevron; or a ring arc with a start angle, a sweep and optional dashes, for dials, tick rings, progress rings and spinners. Circles and capsules are rectangles with half-size radii. Size, corner and border dimensions are independent so resizing preserves authored proportions. The bounded material vocabulary supports solid, linear- and radial-gradient fills, hue and saturation-value colour fields, an optional checker beneath a translucent fill, borders and glow reaching outward and inward from the contour, with ordinary properties and AnimationSystem transitions supplying interaction states; a spinner turns through an ordinary looping clip on its arc's start angle, never through renderer time. Colour fields are evaluated on sRGB-encoded values and decoded per fragment, so the displayed pixel is the colour a picker reports; other fills blend in linear light. Every shape is an analytic distance test without curve evaluation in the one shape program, and the [Canvas publication](../../crates/ipp-core/src/world/systems/canvas/publication.rs) owns their parameters. Complex SVG drawings and arbitrary paths retain the shared quadratic-contour renderer as a specialized path; existing bitmap content remains supported.

A CanvasPaint gives an entity's own box a custom paint: an immutable shader definition whose body computes the fill colour from the fragment's position in the shape's own units, the shape's size and colour and the distance to its contour, with named properties the component owns as a custom material owns its. It fills a CanvasBox or the Background part of the entity's skin or control; the box's colour stays where it is authored and becomes the paint's input, the fill is otherwise solid, and coverage, border, glow, clip and opacity stay the renderer's. RenderService generates the shape program from its static source and the paints in use, rebuilding it when that set changes, so painted primitives batch with ordinary shapes, and property writes update the paint's parameters without regenerating geometry. Paints have no renderer time: they animate through ordinary clips on their properties. A paint that does not compile alone, exceeds the bounded paint or parameter capacity, or lacks an input draws its box's colour and is reported with its entity and reason; the [paint guide](../../crates/ipp-render-gl/src/services/render/CANVAS_PAINTS.md) owns the interface and limits.

RenderService retains derived CPU geometry and GPU batches from stable prepared primitive identities. The shared GL path draws one instanced [record](../../crates/ipp-render-gl/src/services/render/gui_records.rs) per covered quad, with one program for shapes and another for glyphs. A run of consecutive retained work within one layer draws its shapes and then its glyphs, cut where a later shape's paint bounds overlap earlier text, so the result keeps painter order while an ordinary panel takes two draws; the [draw order](../../crates/ipp-render-gl/src/services/render/gui_draw_order.rs) owns the overlap test. Coverage geometry stays tight to paint, including antialiasing and glow. Sparse outlines may omit empty interiors, while filled shapes cover their interiors. Glow expands conservative paint bounds without expanding control hit geometry and remains subject to parent clipping. Material composition follows the common linear-colour and transparency contracts.

Dirty content updates retained CPU geometry and replaces complete bounded GPU batches through storage replacement or available storage; shapes and glyphs keep separate storage, so a text edit rewrites only glyph storage and a shape change only shape storage. Submitted storage remains valid until its GPU consumers finish; reuse cannot assume a fixed number of elapsed frames. Unchanged batches remain reusable, and camera or attachment placement within a stable presentation-quality selection does not regenerate local geometry. RenderService owns allocation, retirement and context recovery independently of authored World state. The Host's RenderService owns retained batches and the single [glyph atlas](../../crates/ipp-render-gl/src/services/render/glyph_atlas.rs); runtime World/output/primitive identity and publication revisions distinguish consumers. Atlas reclamation considers combined presented demand, including frozen branches, under [asset lifetime](assets.md#assets-shared-between-worlds). Detailed buffer layouts, allocation policies and shader algorithms belong beside the implementation.

### Glyph presentation

Repeated text uses renderer-owned glyph image atlases shared across compatible consumers. Glyph quads sample coverage images; font resource identity, glyph identity and rasterization settings distinguish entries. Atlas images are derived from immutable font data and never become authoritative text or client-owned mutable assets. Layout, advances, baselines, caret and selection geometry retain the headless font metrics independently of rasterization choice.

Glyph atlas resolution follows a stable presentation-quality policy; atlas changes cannot reflow text. Live geometry references must remain valid through atlas growth, eviction, font replacement and graphics recovery. Bounded allocations and analytic-curve fallback preserve usable text when a cached glyph is unavailable. Distance-field representations may extend the scaling range after quality and cost evaluation. Direct Surface presentation may use glyph atlases: glyph reuse is independent of caching a whole Surface image and does not delay current text or interaction feedback. The renderer owns atlas memory policy, bounded by device limits and documented heuristics; hosts do not configure per-subsystem budgets.

### Optional Surface texture caching

Direct Canvas presentation through shapes, glyphs, drawings and bitmaps is the default where a Surface provides an exact affine mapping. SurfaceCanvas authoring may opt into texture caching as a presentation optimization; SurfaceCamera already requires a render target. Curved Canvas presentation initially rasterizes content through the same shape, glyph, drawing and bitmap paths and maps it onto renderer-tessellated Surface geometry. Rasterization needed for geometry is independent of optional temporal caching: eligible focus or pointer interaction always presents current content, including current images on a curved Surface. The [distance-based policy](../../crates/ipp-core/src/world/systems/surface/cache_policy.rs) may reduce resolution and refresh frequency for eligible distant content. Stable transitions prevent quality oscillation; frozen branches cannot retain stale interaction priority.

RenderService owns the [derived GPU images](../../crates/ipp-render-gl/src/services/render/surface_cache.rs) and their [refresh scheduling](../../crates/ipp-render-gl/src/services/render/service/surface_cache.rs) from [completed publications](../../crates/ipp-core/src/host/publication.rs) and Host-provided time. Images compose at current placement every rendered frame; reducing optional refresh frequency never slows World evaluation, animation, input or observations. Unchanged content reuses its image. Geometry and placement changes alone do not repaint content unless they change selected raster quality. A rapidly changing affine Surface may return to direct presentation; a curved Surface requiring images refreshes them with current content. Images never become authoritative state or client-authored assets.

Separated image-based layers retain independent images, including content hidden by other layers, so transparency, antialiased edges and content exposed by parallax remain correct. Coincident layers may share one composite image. The renderer handles curved patches and separated layers in view-depth order under ordinary scene depth, transparency and linear-colour rules, including rear views. A single composite image with a topmost-layer mask cannot represent this contract. Image identity includes the exact output lifetime, content, resources and layer grouping; deletion, replacement and graphics recovery cannot reuse another lifetime's image.

Derived images and tessellation use bounded renderer-owned resources and device limits; they may be reclaimed and rebuilt from retained publications under the [asset recovery contract](assets.md#retention-and-recovery). Quality reductions must preserve the selected Surface geometry and its input mapping. Affine presentation can fall back to direct drawing; failure to provide curved presentation is reported as unavailable rather than silently flattening it. Detailed allocation limits, approximation tolerances and refresh heuristics belong beside the implementation.
