# GUI Demo

This gallery world projects a working signal-station dashboard onto one translucent 3D Surface, from a textured metal cube along the shared normal of its recessed lens and the panel. An authored open frustum carries a view-dependent volumetric shader with faint projection rays, sparse dust drifts through it as camera-facing sprites, and a soft studio backdrop, ambient metal fill and an upright baked base and tiled floor light the scene without relighting the moving projection. The dashboard is the showcase of the GUI runtime's built-in looks and the [`@ipp/react/gui-kit`](../../../../packages/ipp-react/README.md#gui-kit) compositions: every control paints the runtime's default skin and does something visible in the panel or the scene. Core layout, paint, hit testing, focus, text editing, control values and scrolling own the interaction; React receives committed callbacks and runs the station.

## Ownership

The gallery World owns the `gui-demo` entity: its Transform, Surface size, cache policy and layer spacing. An [attached World](../../../../docs/architecture/authoring.md#react-host-composition) selects the Canvas, GUI layout, GUI and animation Systems, and its React root declares the dashboard; the Surface presents that World's canvas. The panel authors in the design language's own units, 16-unit body text on a 4-unit grid, and its canvas density of 140 units per metre keeps the Surface at its physical size: the kit draws at its design size, the restated layout in the tests reads like the [language](../../../../tests/skin-lab/README.md#design-language), and body text is about 13 pixels at the authored camera. Removing the page removes the attachment and destroys the panel World with its controllers.

## The dashboard

[dashboard.tsx](dashboard.tsx) composes the page, three columns and the overlays that float above them.

- **TELEMETRY** ([telemetry.tsx](telemetry.tsx)): readouts beside a vertical separator, wrapped operator notes, a segmented choice between EVENTS and SCENE, and a footer with the event count and CLEAR. The body is a ScrollView. EVENTS shows the event log, a VirtualList nested in the body, over the whole history, newest first: the runtime scrolls it by item count and estimate and asks for the items it shows, React declares only those, and each declared entry's measured extent replaces the estimate. A wheel over the log scrolls it until it reaches an end, then the runtime passes the rest outward to the body. CLEAR leaves one entry, the runtime clamps the log's scroll position, and its toast offers UNDO. SCENE shows a tree of the 3D scene's real parts ([scene-tree.ts](scene-tree.ts)): selecting one names it in the FOCUS readout and brightens it in the scene, and the core also lights its trim in the projection colour.
- **SIGNAL MONITOR** ([monitor.tsx](monitor.tsx), [waveform.tsx](waveform.tsx)): the scope and its gain readout, then GAIN, SCAN, PULSE, the callsign editor and UPLINK. Gain drives the projector light, cube energy, wave amplitude and the nodes' signal; SCAN, a switch, scrolls two sine cycles through the scope and holds the uplink; PULSE sends a burst across the scope; PULSE and UPLINK explain themselves in tooltips. The trace and pulse are drawing leaves over immutable Surface curve assets, moved only by Host-owned animation of their Canvas style. Behind them a canvas paint draws the scope's grid or scanlines and a sweep band in the projection colour, which a property clip of the paint carries across the SWEEP range, at first the scope's whole width, at the RATE's speed while SCAN runs; under reduced motion there is no clip and the band stands halfway. The header's SCOPE popover chooses the grid and shows or hides the sweep. Its window controls minimise the panel to its unlit title bar, restore it and close it; a closed monitor's toast reopens it. A minimised or closed monitor keeps its entities hidden, so its controls keep their values and the waveform its controllers.
- **Workbench** ([workbench.tsx](workbench.tsx)): a tabbed panel whose footer, under every tab, holds the node count, SYNC and the amber PURGE. SYNC adds the nodes row by row, and PURGE, behind the input shield, asks in a modal confirmation dialog, then removes them down to the empty state, its toast offering RESTORE.
  - **NODES** ([nodes.tsx](nodes.tsx)): FIND, an autocomplete over the node names whose accepted suggestion scrolls the grid to the node and selects it, and a scrolling data grid, strongest signal first. A press selects a row and focuses its name cell; pressing the selected row again edits the name, which Enter commits. A row's context request, a secondary press or the Menu key, opens its commands: Rename, Ping, which logs the node's answer, and the amber Remove, whose toast restores the node.
  - **CONTROLS** ([tuning-tab.tsx](tuning-tab.tsx), [tuning.ts](tuning.ts)): a scrolling column of the projection's settings. BEAM, a knob with its numeric stepper, scales the beam; LIGHT, a vertical slider with a scale, sets the studio lights; OFFSET, a bipolar slider, moves the scope's traces; SWEEP, a range slider, bounds the sweep band; RATE, a dropdown, sets the scan's speed; PRESET, a searchable dropdown, sets those together; and CHANNELS, a multi-select, switches the beam, the dust and the key and fill lights.
  - **COLOUR** ([colour-tab.tsx](colour-tab.tsx)): the kit's colour picker for the projection colour, which the projector's light, lens, beam and dust and the sweep band take. The picker has a fixed size wider than the workbench at the dashboard's body size, so it draws in a nested kit at three quarters of it.
- **STATUS** ([status.tsx](status.tsx)): badges for the scan, the link and the shield; one inline alert, the most important thing worth saying now (a missing callsign, low gain, the held uplink with its STOP action, or a ready link); the pulse ring, an idle track at rest that follows a burst's crossing; and the operation row, a spinner while an operation connects and then its progress bar and outcome, with the node sync in online and standby parts.
- **ADVANCED** ([advanced.tsx](advanced.tsx)): an expander with ACCENT, EXPLODE LAYERS and REDUCED MOTION.
- **Toasts** report completed actions at the bottom right, above the content; one with an action stays until it is dismissed or its action answers it.

The page's application state is one small [store](store.ts) that the sidebar, rendered by React DOM, and the panel World's own React root both read. Each part of the panel selects the values it shows and re-renders only when they change: moving GAIN re-renders its readouts, the waveform, the node rows and the projector, and nothing else. [scene.tsx](scene.tsx) holds the controls' settings and the event log, [station.ts](station.ts) the station's state and operations, and [tuning.ts](tuning.ts) the projection's settings. The station's operations advance on the Host clock: each step waits for the panel World's next frames and measures elapsed Host time, and their effects are real rows, log entries and toasts.

## Looks and re-theming

Controls without a theme paint the runtime's default looks. Themes appear only where the dashboard shows a variant: the built-in switch look for SCAN and EXPLODE LAYERS, a frameless form of the scroll look for the TELEMETRY body, which its panel already frames, and the kit's amber secondary button for PURGE. PULSE and UPLINK reference one action theme; ACCENT rewrites its rows between the built-in button and amber looks in place, so those controls keep their identities, focus and values, and sets the projection colour to the accent's, which the colour picker then shows. REDUCED MOTION sets the panel World's GUI preference through the root `GuiKit`, which snaps skin transitions and holds the kit's animations; the exploded view then jumps instead of travelling.

## Layers and the exploded view

Every panel lies whole on the canvas's base plane with everything it holds, so a panel never comes apart. Only the kit's overlays float, each on its own [plane](../../../../docs/architecture/rendering.md#surface-presentation) named by `GUI_KIT_LAYERS`: anchored overlays (the dropdown and suggestion lists, the SCOPE popover, tooltips and the node rows' context menu) on the anchored plane, PURGE's dialog on the dialog plane and the toast stack on the toast plane. A plane's depth is its id times the Surface's layer spacing whichever planes are in use, so each overlay always stands at the same depth; the spacing separates the anchored plane visibly while the toasts' plane stays in frame. EXPLODE LAYERS, in the panel or the sidebar, plays an ordinary animation of that spacing on the Host clock, forward to separate the planes and backward to close them; changing the spacing regenerates no geometry. Input meets each plane nearest first, so the exploded panel and its open overlays stay usable. The [input shield](shield.tsx) guards PURGE on the base plane, so it stays in place when the overlays lift.

## Panel presentation

The panel opts into [whole-Surface texture caching](../../../../docs/architecture/rendering.md#optional-surface-texture-caching) through `<SurfaceCache>` on the `gui-demo` entity in [scene.tsx](scene.tsx). The authored camera is about 16.7 m from the panel centre, inside the policy's 20 m direct distance, so the default view and every close interaction draw directly. Zooming out past about 22 m presents the panel from a cached image at 80 texels per Surface metre and at most 15 repaints per second; each further distance doubling halves both. **Panel presentation** selects Automatic caching, Cached at every distance (for comparing against Direct rendering at an identical camera) or Direct rendering. One flat image cannot show separated planes, so an exploded panel presents directly in every mode, and the cache serves it again once it is flat; the FPS readout shows the difference. Hover, press, drag and focus also draw the panel directly.

**Isolate GUI panel only** unmounts the projector, floor, backdrop, lights, beam and input shield while keeping the Surface, its panel World and the camera, so the FPS readout measures the whole Surface workload. The input shield is scene geometry with picking geometry; while armed, the scene names its exact picking geometry in `IppCanvas.guiInput.blockers`, which the canvas applies when it opens its physical input context, so a shield mounted later is not yet marked there. **Lift input shield** greys the shield's frame and stops naming it. Visual occlusion alone never blocks GUI input.

## Input, assets and validation

The canvas's physical input context routes gestures that start on the panel to the GUI; pointer and wheel input the runtime leaves unhandled controls the gallery camera, each gesture keeping its first owner across panel boundaries. The browser adapter prevents the browser's context menu over the canvas; the gallery camera has no secondary-button gesture. Reset camera restores the authored view.

The gallery build exports the packed [Blender projector](authoring/PROVENANCE.md) without rebaking, converts the two waveform SVGs through the shared Surface converter and uses the [shared font](../../../../assets/fonts/README.md) in full: the callsign editor accepts text beyond the labels on screen, and a subset would need an explicit character repertoire, attribution and editable-text coverage.

`python tools/ipp.py test gallery-gui gallery-gui-camera` runs the [dashboard](../../../../tests/render/gallery-gui.test.ts), [workbench](../../../../tests/render/gallery-gui-workbench.test.ts) and [camera](../../../../tests/render/gallery-gui-camera.test.ts) suites. They read control state from the panel World's fields, locate controls through an [independent restatement](../../../../tests/render/gallery-gui-panel.ts) of the layout, drive them with real pointer and keyboard input, and check completed frames: layout, label, scroll and text geometry against that restatement, the effects of the controls on the panel and the scene, the shield, cached against direct presentation, whole panels on the base plane and open overlays on their own planes when exploded, with presses landing on each plane, and the tooltip, popover, lists, context menu and confirmation dialog. See the [gallery guide](../../README.md) for building and running the world.
