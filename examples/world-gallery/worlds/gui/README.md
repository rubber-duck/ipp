# GUI Demo

This gallery world presents a settings panel on one translucent 3D Surface, projected from a textured metal cube along the shared normal of its recessed lens and the panel. An authored open frustum carries a view-dependent volumetric shader with sparse drifting dust and faint projection rays; a soft gray studio backdrop, ambient metal fill, and an upright baked base and tiled floor provide fixed illumination without relighting the moving projection. Core layout, paint, hit testing, focus, text editing, control values and scrolling own the interaction semantics; React receives committed callbacks to update the explanatory sidebar and the surrounding scene.

The panel is ordinary Canvas and GUI content of its own World. The gallery World owns the `gui-demo` entity: its Transform, Surface size and cache policy. An [attached World](../../../../docs/architecture/authoring.md#react-host-composition) selects the Canvas, GUI layout, GUI and animation Systems, and its React root declares the Canvas, layout containers, text and drawing leaves, controls and theme entities; the parent Surface presents that World's Canvas through an explicit output. Surface size and placement belong to the gallery World, Canvas density and content to the panel World. Removing the page removes the attachment and destroys the panel World with its controllers.

Controls reference five theme entities and paint retained rounded boxes with borders, gradients and glow from their part rows. PULSE fills with a radial gradient centred on its icon, and Neon adds resting halos around the controls. The panel shell, frames and SPAN's header frame are plain rounded Canvas boxes; SPAN resizes the header frame, filled with a dimmed accent so it stands out from the shell, while its corner radii keep their authored units. UPLINK stays disabled while SCAN runs and paints the skin's disabled lane. Skin selection rewrites the theme rows in place, preserving control identities, focus, committed values and animation controllers. Each theme also carries an ordinary `GuiThemeMotion` table that the scene writes through its observation session of the panel World, so appearance changes transition through the selected skin's motion clip and the SCAN switch knob glides between its ends.

Dashboard, signal, pulse and skin icons are text leaves in the bundled Shure Tech Mono Nerd Font, sized by font size alone; the font centres each icon's ink in its intrinsic advance-by-line box, so the alignment in each fixed cell places the ink itself. Decorations are offset by leading Stack margins. The scrolling sine trace, independent pulse and reference grid are drawing leaves over immutable Surface curve assets in a clipped viewport. Only the two trace entities move, through Host-owned animation of their Canvas style translation in the panel World; gain changes their scale without rewriting their geometry.

The panel doubles as the framework's settings-panel specimen. The TELEMETRY view is a ScrollView holding the readouts, operator notes that wrap at word boundaries into several lines of a fixed-width Text leaf, and the event log nested inside it. The event log is a VirtualList over the demo's event history, newest first: nearly a hundred archived entries plus every command the panel records. The runtime scrolls it by the item count and a per-item extent estimate, asks for the items it shows plus two on either side, and React declares only those entries; the sidebar shows the declared range. Short entries fit one line and longer ones wrap into two, and each declared entry's measured extent replaces the estimate. The estimate is the taller extent, so measuring entries only shortens the content and dragging the thumb to the end settles at the last entry. Both views show themed vertical scroll bars; drag a thumb or page along a track to scroll. A wheel over the event log scrolls the log until it reaches an end, then the runtime passes the remaining movement outward to the telemetry view. PURGE clears the log to a single entry; the runtime clamps the log's scroll position to what remains. It sits behind a hatched amber [input shield](shield.tsx): scene geometry with picking geometry in front of the panel. While armed, the scene names the shield's exact picking geometry in `IppCanvas.guiInput.blockers`; the canvas applies blockers when it opens its physical input context, so a shield mounted later is not yet marked there. **Lift input shield** in the scene controls greys the shield's frame and stops naming it while leaving it in place. Visual occlusion alone never blocks GUI input, so an unmarked shield lets PURGE take clicks through the glass.

Signal gain changes the projector light, cube energy and waveform amplitude. Autoscan scrolls two sine cycles through the stationary graph. The SCAN checkbox is themed as a capsule switch: its indicator's alignment moves the knob to the left or right end of the track. PULSE adds an independent bright pulse with a flat baseline, including while scanning is paused. The Host owns both animation clocks and the separate 3D dust phase. [dashboard.tsx](dashboard.tsx) composes the panel and its themes, [presentation.tsx](presentation.tsx) owns the layout, shape, text and theme-row helpers and verified glyph choices, and [waveform.tsx](waveform.tsx) owns the trace entities and their animation clips. [scene.tsx](scene.tsx) coordinates readiness, the attached panel World and skin motion, [projector.tsx](projector.tsx) supplies the 3D assembly, [shield.tsx](shield.tsx) places the input shield, and [controls.tsx](controls.tsx) exposes the committed state in the sidebar.

The gallery build exports the packed [Blender projector](authoring/PROVENANCE.md) without rebaking and uses the [shared font](../../../../assets/fonts/README.md). Control geometry is authored directly in the application; the build converts the three waveform SVG assets through the shared Surface converter.

The GUI demo intentionally keeps the complete maintained font: 11,307 glyphs and 11,306 mapped code points in the 2,336,172-byte source, producing the 9,126,548-byte transport fixture. Its callsign editor accepts user text beyond the labels currently on screen, and the SIL Open Font License requires a subset to remain a properly attributed modified font under a non-reserved name. Reducing the asset to current static labels would silently narrow editable-text coverage, so startup work must improve transport and preparation without shrinking this large-font fixture. A future subset would need an explicit supported character repertoire, generated attribution and editable-text coverage tests.

The canvas's physical input context routes gestures that start on the panel to the GUI; pointer and wheel input the runtime leaves unhandled controls the gallery camera. Each gesture keeps its initial owner across panel boundaries. The browser adapter forwards DOM wheel deltas to the runtime in Canvas units. Use Reset camera to restore the authored view. See the [gallery guide](../../README.md) for building and validating the world.

Use **Isolate GUI panel only** in the scene controls to unmount the projector, floor, backdrop, lights, beam and input shield while retaining the GUI Surface, its panel World and the camera. The FPS readout stays visible; compare it at the same camera angle after the rate settles, then use **Restore full scene** to remount the assembly. The isolated view still includes GUI text, backgrounds, controls and waveform animation, so it measures the complete Surface workload rather than a single rendering primitive.

The panel opts into [whole-Surface texture caching](../../../../docs/architecture/rendering.md#optional-surface-texture-caching) through `<SurfaceCache>` on the `gui-demo` entity in [scene.tsx](scene.tsx). The demo is one Surface presenting one Canvas, so the header, controls, waveform and telemetry share one cached image; splitting it into several Surfaces would change the specimen, and GUI interaction already returns the whole panel to direct drawing while it is hovered, pressed, dragged or focused. The authored camera is about 16.7 m from the panel centre, inside the policy's 20 m direct distance, so the default view and every close interaction draw directly. Zooming the camera out past about 22 m (the boundary plus hysteresis) presents the panel from a cached image at 80 texels per content metre and at most 15 repaints per second; each further distance doubling halves both. The waveform changes paint on every frame while it scans, so a cap near the display rate would repaint the whole image every frame, which costs more than drawing the panel directly; half that rate keeps the distant trace readable. The density approximates the on-screen density of a 720-pixel-high canvas at the boundary; high-DPR screens see softer distant text, which is the intended distant-quality reduction.

**Panel presentation** selects Automatic caching, Cached at every distance (for comparing the authored view against Direct rendering at an identical camera) or Direct rendering, which removes the opt-in. Combine it with **Isolate GUI panel only** and the FPS readout for a practical visual comparison; the FPS readout is not GPU timing. The `gallery-gui` suite reads control state from the panel World's control fields and locates controls with an independent restatement of the panel layout. It checks the settings panel on completed frames: each wrapped notes line paints ink up to its last glyph, thumbs paint where an independent scroll bar calculation places them before and after nested scrolling, the event log's loaded range and capacity match an independent layout of its declared entries after a wheel step, a thumb drag to the end and PURGE, with entry ink on their wrapped lines, and lifting the shield changes pixels only inside its projected box, with expected, actual and difference images under `target/integration-artifacts/gallery-gui/settings-panel`. It also records the selected mode, band, image size, repaints and resident bytes from the renderer's read-only diagnostics, and compares cached and direct captures of the isolated panel under the same camera and viewport in two skins. `gallery-gui-camera` shows that orbiting over the cached panel repaints and uploads nothing, and that dollying across band boundaries resizes the image once per band.
